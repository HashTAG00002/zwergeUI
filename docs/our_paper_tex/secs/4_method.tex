\section{\textsc{ZwerGe-UI}}
\label{sec:method}

The analysis in \S\ref{sec:analysis} reveals that the richest spatial representations reside at intermediate transformer layers, where the instruction-conditioned spatial posterior peaks before being overwritten by coordinate serialization.
\textsc{ZwerGe-UI} retrofits existing GUI agents to read this intermediate-layer signal without modifying the backbone.
The Stage~1 per-layer probes trained here also serve as the readout heads used in all probe experiments in \S\ref{sec:analysis}.
Figure~\ref{fig:zwerge_pipeline} provides an overview of this retrofit pipeline, connecting the prefill-time layerwise readout, per-layer spatial posteriors, and cross-layer fusion used in the following subsections.


% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Design Rationale}
\label{sec:method:rationale}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{A dedicated readout head.}
The raw hidden states at intermediate layers \emph{contain} spatial information (Finding 1) but are not calibrated for direct readout.
A full-rank cross-attention probe trained on grounding examples with the backbone frozen is sufficient to decode this latent signal; it introduces no new spatial knowledge into the backbone.

\paragraph{Prefill-forced inference.}
Coordinate generation serializes location into up to eight individual number tokens, but the spatial intent is already encoded before decoding begins (Finding 2).
Because the backbone is frozen, we prefill a fixed template ending in an anchor token:
\begin{equation*}
  \texttt{click(}
  \underbrace{\texttt{<|ground|>}}_{\text{anchor}}
  \underbrace{\texttt{<|pointer\_start|>}\cdots}_{\text{coord}}
\end{equation*}
and run a \textbf{single forward pass}.
At the anchor position, the model has processed the full screenshot and instruction but no coordinate value, so there is zero label leakage.

\paragraph{RMSNorm pre-scale.}
Hidden-state $\ell_2$ norms grow substantially with layer depth due to the geometrically misaligned activation spaces across layers.
We apply a pre-scale $\mathbf{h} \leftarrow \mathbf{h} / (\|\mathbf{h}\|_2 / \sqrt{d})$ before each LayerNorm, bounding the input norm to ${\approx}\sqrt{d}$ regardless of depth and preventing numerical overflow in \texttt{bfloat16}. This also explains the heterogeneity of the semantic spaces handled by different layers from the perspective of activation norm.

\paragraph{Two training stages.}
Stage~1 trains one independent probe per layer with the backbone frozen, sufficient both for the probe experiments in \S\ref{sec:analysis} and as a standalone grounding system.
Stage~2 loads the Stage~1 checkpoint and trains a lightweight cross-layer fusion head on top of the frozen probes.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 1: Independent Per-Layer Probes}
\label{sec:method:stage1}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Setup.}
Let $f_\theta$ be a frozen backbone with $L$ layers.
Given input $\mathbf{x}$ (screenshot $+$ instruction $+$ prefill template), we extract per-layer hidden states via forward hooks.
For each probe layer $\ell \in \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{probe}}$ (the deeper half of the backbone, where spatial signal concentrates), we read the anchor query $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{t^*} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ at the \texttt{<|ground|>} position and visual keys $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times d}$ over the $N_v$ patch tokens. This corresponds to the layerwise branches in Figure~\ref{fig:zwerge_pipeline}, where each selected transformer layer independently maps the anchor token and visual patch tokens into a spatial heatmap over the screen.

\paragraph{Cross-attention probe.}
Each probe $\phi_\ell$ computes a patch-level spatial posterior via multi-head cross-attention:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{Q}_\ell 
  &= W_q^{(\ell)}\,\mathrm{Norm}\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{t^*}\right), \\
  \mathbf{K}_\ell 
  &= W_k^{(\ell)}\,\mathrm{Norm}\!\left(\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}_v\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Norm}(\cdot)$ denotes the RMS pre-scale followed by LayerNorm, and $W_q^{(\ell)}, W_k^{(\ell)}$ are full-rank projections (no bottleneck), letting each probe learn a fresh geometry suited to its layer.
Let $\sigma(\cdot)$ denote the softmax operation. Per-head scores are combined via a learnable gate $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$:
\begin{equation}
  p_\ell = \sigma\!\left(\sum_h \sigma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_h \cdot \frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell,h}^\top \mathbf{K}_{\ell,h}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right) \in \Delta^{N_v-1}.
  \label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
Each probe contributes ${\approx}4.2$M parameters ($d{=}4096$, $n_h{=}8$, $d_h{=}64$), and the full probe set totals ${\approx}42$M parameters, or $0.52\%$ of the 7B backbone (Qwen2.5-VL). For Qwen3-VL, this ratio is of the same order of magnitude.

\paragraph{Supervision.}
Each probe is supervised with an anisotropic Gaussian label centered at the bounding-box centroid.
For patch $i$ at position $(x_i, y_i)$:
\begin{equation}
  y_i \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{(x_i{-}c_x)^2}{2\sigma_x^2} - \frac{(y_i{-}c_y)^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\right),
  \label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_x {=} \eta w_b$, $\sigma_y {=} \eta h_b$, $\eta{=}0.35$.
This soft label reduces over-penalization from patch-grid quantization on small high-resolution targets (Finding 4), and the variance $\sigma_x, \sigma_y$ is computed per sample and embedded inline rather than treated as a global constant.
Stage~1 minimizes:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S1}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{probe}}|}\sum_\ell D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{y} \| p_\ell).
  \label{eq:loss_s1}
\end{equation}
Gradients from $\ell$ affect only $\phi_\ell$; the backbone receives zero gradient throughout.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 2: Cross-Layer Fusion}
\label{sec:method:stage2}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Stage~1 produces independent per-layer posteriors whose quality varies non-monotonically across depth (Finding 1), and the optimal layer differs by sample.
A static average is suboptimal.
Stage~2 trains a cross-layer fusion head to \emph{select and weight} per-layer posteriors conditioned on their content, with the backbone and the inactive probe layers frozen; the fusion head and the active-subset probes (the spatial-plateau layers) remain trainable. As illustrated in the right part of Figure~\ref{fig:zwerge_pipeline}, these layerwise heatmaps are not averaged uniformly; instead, the fusion module assigns input-dependent weights to recover the most reliable spatial posterior.

\paragraph{Active probe layers.}
We use a subset $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{active}} \subset \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{probe}}$ drawn from the spatial plateau identified in \S\ref{sec:analysis:layerwise} as inputs to fusion; the remaining probe layers are retained in the checkpoint but not fused.

\paragraph{Fusion head.}
The fusion head processes anchor representations $\{\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{t^*}\}_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{active}}}$.
A lightweight cross-layer LoRA module exchanges information across depths and produces refined queries $\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_\ell$.
A cosine-meta scorer then produces a per-layer weight:
 
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{final}} = \sum_\ell \sigma(\mathrm{score})_\ell\cdot p_\ell.
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
The term $\alpha_\ell$ is a sample-independent trainable prior capturing the average quality of layer $\ell$ across the training distribution.
The cosine term computes a sample-dependent posterior: $\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{meta}}$ is a global trainable meta-query acting as a soft prototype for the ideal grounding representation, and $\tau$ is a learnable temperature; layers whose refined query aligns with $\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{meta}}$ receive higher weight for the current input.
All fusion parameters initialize so that weights are uniform at the start of training.
The cross-layer LoRA contributes ${\approx}200$K parameters in total, two orders of magnitude fewer than a single probe.

\paragraph{Stage 2 training.}
Stage~2 loads the Stage-1 checkpoint. The backbone and the inactive probe layers are frozen; the fusion head and the active-subset probes in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{active}}$ remain trainable.
The loss combines a top-level KL term on $p_{\mathrm{final}}$ with a per-layer auxiliary:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S2}} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{y} \| p_{\mathrm{final}}) + \frac{\lambda}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{active}}|}\sum_\ell D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{y} \| p_\ell),
  \label{eq:loss_s2}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda{=}0.2$.
Each active probe receives a direct gradient from the auxiliary term in addition to the indirect gradient it receives via $p_{\mathrm{final}}$ in the fusion loss, so no single probe is neglected merely because the fusion scorer assigns it a low $\omega_\ell$; this prevents collapse onto a single dominant layer while keeping all active probe posteriors individually calibrated.


% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Training-Free Posterior-to-Point Decoding}
\label{sec:method:p2p}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The fusion head outputs a patch posterior $p_{\mathrm{final}}(i)$ over visual tokens, not a coordinate; the legacy decoder --- pick the connected component with the largest single-patch probability and take its weighted centroid --- both ranks regions by a single peak (ignoring most of the posterior mass) and discards the sub-patch information encoded in the unthresholded Gaussian neighbourhood.
We convert $p_{\mathrm{final}}$ into a single strict click with a parameter-free, training-free posterior-to-point (P2P) decoder built from three composable stages.

\paragraph{Level 1: region re-ranking.}
Thresholding $p_{\mathrm{final}}$ at $0.3\max$ and 4-connectivity BFS yields candidate regions $\{C_j\}$. We score each by its total posterior mass normalised by $\sqrt{|C_j|}$, $S_j = \sum_{i\in C_j} p_{\mathrm{final}}(i)/\sqrt{|C_j|}$, a compromise between mass (which favours large regions) and mean (which favours isolated spikes), replacing the peak-only score used previously.

\paragraph{Level 2: cross-layer consensus.}
The same forward pass already exposes $\{p_\ell\}$ and fusion weights $\omega_\ell$. For each dilated candidate $\widetilde C_j$, we compute the per-layer region mass $m_{\ell j}=\sum_{i\in\widetilde C_j} p_\ell(i)$ and the $\omega$-weighted geometric mean $S_j^{\rm cons}=\sum_\ell \omega_\ell\log(m_{\ell j}{+}\epsilon)$, which penalises peaks supported by only one layer --- exactly the fusion-noise artefacts a convex average can amplify.

\paragraph{Level 3: local Gaussian inversion.}
For the winning region we recover a sub-patch mode by fitting a weighted quadratic to $z=\log(p_{\mathrm{final}}{+}\epsilon)$ on a $5{\times}5$ neighbourhood of the peak and solving $\Delta^{*}=-H^{-1}g$; this inverts the anisotropic Gaussian geometry the probes were trained to emit. We apply it only to small ($|C|{\le}2$) regions --- the sub-patch-quantization regime --- and fall back to the centroid whenever the Hessian is not negative-definite, the fit is ill-conditioned, or the offset exceeds a $0.25$-cell clamp, bounding the damage rate on already-correct samples.

\paragraph{Posterior-constrained native decoding.}
For backbones whose own coordinate serializer is already precise, we additionally gate the native autoregressive coordinate: we emit it iff it lands inside a ZwerGe top-$k$ region, otherwise we emit the P2P point. This assigns each component its strongest role --- the intermediate posterior decides \emph{which} region is trustworthy, the native decoder supplies the \emph{continuous} point --- and instantiates the paper's central claim that intermediate layers identify \emph{where} while final layers serialize \emph{how to say} it.
