\section{Related Work}

\subsection{GUI Agent Grounding} 
\label{ssec: GUIG}
GUI grounding maps user instructions to executable screen elements or locations, serving as a core perception capability for GUI agents. Compared with general visual grounding, GUI grounding faces unique challenges from dense text, small icons, regular layouts, high-resolution screenshots, and platform-specific interaction semantics. Early GUI-oriented agents and datasets, such as META-GUI~\citep{sun2022metagui}, connect task-oriented dialogue with real mobile GUI operations, showing that conversational agents can execute tasks on real apps without relying on task-specific backend APIs. Representative screenshot-based GUI agents and UI-centric MLLMs, such as CogAgent~\citep{hong2024cogagent}, SeeClick~\citep{cheng2024seeclick}, Ferret-UI~\citep{you2024ferretui}, and OmniParser~\citep{lu2024omniparser}, demonstrate that visual grounding can reduce reliance on HTML, DOM, or accessibility trees. Recent works further improve GUI grounding by scaling model capacity, GUI data, and action traces, including UGround~\citep{gou2024navigating}, OS-Atlas~\citep{Os-atlas}, ShowUI~\citep{lin2025showui}, Aguvis~\citep{xu2024aguvis}, and UI-TARS~\citep{qin2025uitars}. Meanwhile, ScreenSpot-Pro~\citep{screenspotpro} shows that professional high-resolution GUI grounding remains challenging, especially for small targets, complex layouts, and fine-grained manipulation.

Most existing GUI grounding methods formulate localization as coordinate or action-parameter prediction. To improve this paradigm, recent works introduce GUI-specific reinforcement or reward signals, such as UI-R1~\citep{lu2025ui}, GUI-R1~\citep{luo2025gui}, InfiGUI-R1~\citep{liu2025infigui}, GUI-G1~\citep{zhou2025gui}, and InfiGUI-G1~\citep{liu2026infigui}. However, coordinate-based grounding may suffer from unstable numerical prediction and ambiguous point-level supervision, motivating coordinate-free, attention-based, or region-centric alternatives. Complementary to direct coordinate prediction, layout-aware GUI referring methods such as Tree-of-Lens~\citep{fan2024readpointed} construct hierarchical layout structures from screenshots and user-indicated points, highlighting the importance of region semantics and spatial relationships for interpretable GUI understanding. Along this line, TAG~\citep{xu2025attention}, GUI-Actor~\citep{wu2025gui}, RegionFocus~\citep{luo2025regionfocus}, DiMo-GUI~\citep{wu2025dimogui}, and FocusUI~\citep{ouyang2026focusui} explore intrinsic attention mining, action-region alignment, visual test-time scaling, modality-aware refinement, or position-preserving token selection. Concurrently, Re-Prefill~\citep{lin2026happens} shows that the prefill stage largely determines candidate UI elements before coordinate decoding, and improves grounding via an attention-guided second prefill. This work shares our motivation that GUI grounding should not be viewed merely as autoregressive coordinate generation. However, Re-Prefill relies on raw cross-layer attention to construct target hypotheses and revisits inference with an additional prefill stage, whereas our method learns a lightweight frozen-backbone grounding head to explicitly compute token-region similarity and dynamically fuse task-relevant cues, providing a more direct, calibrated, and interpretable coordinate-free readout.



\subsection{Visual Transformers Grounding}
\label{ssec: ViTG}
Visual Transformers provide the architectural foundation for modeling language, vision, and spatial correspondence in modern grounding systems. ViT~\citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} introduces patch-level visual tokenization, while CLIP~\citep{radford2021clip} aligns visual and textual representations through large-scale contrastive pretraining. Building on these foundations, DETR~\citep{carion2020detr}, MDETR~\citep{kamath2021mdetr}, GLIP~\citep{li2022glip}, Grounding DINO~\citep{liu2023groundingdino}, OWL-ViT~\citep{minderer2022owlvit}, and SAM~\citep{kirillov2023sam} extend detection toward phrase grounding, open-vocabulary localization, and promptable segmentation. More recently, grounded MLLMs such as Kosmos-2~\citep{peng2023kosmos2}, Shikra~\citep{chen2023shikra}, Ferret~\citep{you2023ferret}, Qwen-VL~\citep{bai2023qwenvl}, and Qwen2.5-VL~\citep{bai2025qwen25vl} unify region understanding, coordinate prediction, and natural language generation, making grounding a general capability of multimodal instruction-following models.
Despite their strong grounding ability, Transformer-based VLMs face efficiency and explainability challenges. On the efficiency side, high-resolution images introduce many visual tokens and substantially increase attention cost; therefore, efficient VLM and token compression methods such as ViLT~\citep{kim2021vilt}, PuMer~\citep{cao2023pumer}, FastV~\citep{chen2024fastv}, and TokenPacker~\citep{li2024tokenpacker} reduce visual redundancy through lightweight encoders, pruning, merging, or compressed visual projectors. On the explainability side, Attention Rollout~\citep{abnar2020attentionflow} and Transformer relevance propagation~\citep{chefer2021transformer} attempt to identify visual evidence behind Transformer predictions. However, attention is not always a faithful explanation: attention sink in LLMs~\citep{xiao2023streamingllm}, register tokens in ViTs~\citep{darcet2024registers}, and visual attention sink in LMMs~\citep{kang2025visualsink} show that high-attention or high-norm tokens may serve internal computation rather than correspond to task-relevant regions. These findings suggest that efficient and explainable grounding should not simply prune or interpret tokens by raw attention scores, but should explicitly preserve spatial evidence and distinguish task-relevant visual regions from redundant or sink-like tokens.
