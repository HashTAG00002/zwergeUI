\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments:setup}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on five GUI grounding benchmarks spanning desktop, web, and mobile platforms.
\textbf{ScreenSpot-Pro}~\citep{screenspotpro} is a high-resolution desktop grounding benchmark with fine-grained domain categories (Development, Creative, CAD, Scientific, Office, OS); it is the primary benchmark for our analysis (\S\ref{sec:analysis}) and main results.
\textbf{ScreenSpot-v2}~\citep{Os-atlas} covers mobile, desktop, and web screenshots at moderate resolution.
\textbf{OSWorld-G}~\citep{xie2025scaling} provides GUI grounding samples drawn from real computer task executions, categorized by task type (Text Matching, Element Recognition, Layout Understanding, Fine-grained Manipulation). We remove refusal samples from the evaluation set and report results on the remaining non-refusal subset.
\textbf{MMBench-GUI}~\citep{mmbench} is a multi-platform benchmark covering Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, and Web.
\textbf{UI-Vision}~\citep{ui-vision} evaluates grounding across Basic, Functional, Spatial, and Layout task types; we report the weighted average (Avg) over all 5{,}790 samples.



\paragraph{Metrics.}
We use two metrics, both made explicit so that no comparison conflates them.
\textbf{overlap@$k$} counts a prediction correct if a $1{\times}1$-patch box centred at the predicted point (half-width $0.5$ patch, i.e.\ $14$--$16$\,px depending on backbone patch size) intersects the ground-truth box, taking the best of the top-$k$ candidates; \textbf{hit@$k$} is the stricter point-strictly-inside-the-box variant used on public leaderboards.
Our dense coordinate-free head exposes multiple disconnected spatial modes in a single prefill, so we report \textbf{overlap@3 as single-pass proposal recall} --- an upper bound on what the posterior could localize in one forward --- rather than as executable accuracy. Unlike autoregressive sampling, increasing the proposal budget requires no additional image forwards or coordinate-token decoding.
To convert proposal recall into a single executable action, we introduce a parameter-free \emph{posterior-to-point} (P2P) decoder (\S\ref{sec:method:p2p}) that (i) re-ranks candidate regions by $\omega$-weighted cross-layer posterior consensus and (ii) reconstructs a continuous local mode from the unthresholded Gaussian posterior.
\textbf{All final \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} action results are reported under strict top-1 overlap@1 and hit@1}, the same protocol as the autoregressive baseline; for protocol symmetry, overlap@1 dilates both the baseline's autoregressive coordinate and the ZwerGe point by the same half-cell support, so the two are directly comparable.
overlap@3 is reported separately (Table~\ref{tab:proposal_recall}) and is never substituted for top-1 executable accuracy; the gap between overlap@3 and overlap@1 measures exactly the headroom the P2P decoder aims to recover.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We retrofit \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} onto four GUI agents: \textbf{GUI-Owl-7B}~\citep{ye2025guiowl} and \textbf{UI-TARS-1.5-7B}~\citep{qin2025uitars} (Qwen2.5-VL backbone, 28 layers) and \textbf{GUI-Owl-1.5-8B}~\citep{xu2026mobile} and \textbf{UI-Venus-1.5-8B}~\citep{team2026ui} (Qwen3-VL backbone, 36 layers). Each backbone is kept completely frozen throughout both training stages; only the probe and fusion heads are updated.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Stage 1 (A7, \S\ref{sec:method:stage1}) trains 14–18 independent CrossAttn probes per model on 200k grounding samples for 1 epoch. Stage 2 (A8, \S\ref{sec:method:stage2}) loads the Stage 1 checkpoint and trains the Cross-Layer Fusion head together with the active-subset probes (12 active layers, fusion\_lora\_rank=128, context\_rank=64, $\lambda{=}0.2$) for 1 epoch on the same data, with the backbone and inactive probes frozen. Inference uses the BFS centroid decode strategy with a $0.3{\times}\max$ threshold; no autoregressive decoding is performed.

Tables~\ref{tab:main_sspro}–\ref{tab:main_uivision} report overlap@1 for baseline rows and overlap@3 for \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} rows (\S\ref{sec:experiments:setup}), for all four backbone models.
Each backbone occupies two adjacent rows: the original model (baseline, overlap@1) followed by its \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} retrofitted version (\textbf{+ ZwerGe}, overlap@3).
Because the two rows use different protocols, the absolute deltas discussed below conflate the retrofit itself with the relaxation from a single autoregressive prediction to the best of three patch-posterior candidates read from one forward pass, and should not be read as an ablation of the retrofit in isolation.
Since \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} is an incremental retrofit rather than a standalone agent, we do not include external leaderboard entries in the main comparison — the primary question is whether retrofitting a frozen backbone with our grounding heads provides a consistent improvement under this protocol.

\textbf{Qwen2.5-VL backbones benefit more than Qwen3-VL backbones.}
The overlap@3 proposal-recall numbers above (e.g.\ GUI-Owl-7B $+16.82$\,pp, GUI-Owl-1.5-8B $+1.77$\,pp on SS-Pro) over-state the executable-accuracy gain: under the strict overlap@1 protocol (Table~\ref{tab:proposal_recall}), the same Qwen3-VL backbones are in fact slightly \emph{negative} (GUI-Owl-1.5-8B $-6.08$\,pp, UI-Venus-1.5-8B $-4.17$\,pp on SS-Pro) --- the proposal-recall ceiling had masked this.
This is consistent with our hypothesis rather than a contradiction of it: Qwen3-VL models are trained on substantially more diverse coordinate-serializing supervision, so their native final layer already approaches the intermediate-layer ceiling and there is no headroom for a coordinate-free retrofit to exploit.
We make this quantitative in \S\ref{sec:experiments:severity} (Figure~\ref{fig:bottleneck_severity}) --- the retrofit gain is a positive function of the peak-probe-minus-native \emph{headroom}, which is positive for Qwen2.5-VL and negative for Qwen3-VL --- and recover the gap with the posterior-constrained native-decoding gate in \S\ref{sec:experiments:p2p}.

\textbf{High-resolution layouts remain a challenge for patch-level decoding.}
ScreenSpot-Pro is the most demanding benchmark because targets are often single-pixel or sub-icon elements on $2{,}560{\times}1{,}440$ or larger screenshots.
\textsc{ZwerGe-UI} replaces pixel-precise coordinate regression with a patch-level posterior over the visual token grid, so the minimum localization granularity is one patch rather than a single pixel.
On ScreenSpot-Pro, several domain categories (CAD, Creative) show the smallest gains even under the top-3 relaxation, whereas moderate-resolution benchmarks (ScreenSpot-v2, MMBench-GUI) are more uniformly positive.
The patch granularity is an architectural trade-off: it enables frozen-backbone readout without any coordinate vocabulary but introduces a resolution ceiling that limits gains on the hardest high-resolution domains, a ceiling that additional candidates alone cannot fully remove.

\textbf{Spatial grounding tasks benefit most.}
UI-Vision decomposes grounding into Basic, Functional, Spatial, and Layout subtypes.
Baseline models are weakest on Spatial (8.73--40.41\%), and \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} provides the largest absolute improvements there: GUI-Owl-7B improves Spatial from 11.52 to 46.25\% ($+34.73$~pp) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B from 8.73 to 30.08\% ($+21.35$~pp).
This is the task type most directly probing whether a model has formed a spatial posterior over the screen, which is precisely the signal read by our intermediate-layer probes.

\subsection{Bottleneck Severity Predicts the Retrofit Gain}
\label{sec:experiments:severity}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mixed Qwen3-VL results (Table~\ref{tab:proposal_recall}) are not a method failure but a falsifiable prediction of the bottleneck hypothesis.
Define the \emph{retrofit headroom} of a backbone as the gap between its best intermediate-layer probe overlap@1 and its native autoregressive baseline overlap@1: $h = \max_\ell \text{ov@1}(p_\ell) - \text{ov@1}(\text{native})$.
When $h>0$, the intermediate posterior is more spatially accurate than what the final-layer serializer emits, so bypassing the serializer recovers headroom; when $h \le 0$, the native decoder already matches or exceeds the intermediate ceiling and the retrofit has nothing to exploit.

Figure~\ref{fig:bottleneck_severity} scatter-plots $h$ against the strict-protocol ZwerGe delta ($\text{+Z ov@1} - \text{Base ov@1}$, fair, same protocol) across all four backbones and five benchmarks.
Qwen2.5-VL backbones (blue) occupy the positive-headroom / positive-gain quadrant; Qwen3-VL backbones (red) occupy the negative-headroom / negative-gain quadrant.
The correlation is mechanical, not coincidental: GUI-Owl-1.5-8B and UI-Venus-1.5-8B are trained on substantially more diverse coordinate-serializing supervision, so their native final layer already approaches the intermediate-layer ceiling --- exactly the regime where \emph{no} coordinate-free retrofit can help, by construction.
Reporting overlap@3 had masked this (e.g.\ GUI-Owl-1.5-8B on SS-Pro shows $+1.96$\,pp at overlap@3 but $-6.08$\,pp at strict overlap@1); the strict protocol makes the null result legible, and the headroom axis explains it. The P2P decoder (\S\ref{sec:method:p2p}), and in particular its posterior-constrained native-decoding gate, targets precisely the $h{\le}0$ Qwen3 points: it inherits the native decoder's sub-patch precision whenever the ZwerGe posterior endorses the same region.

\subsection{Posterior-to-Point Decoder Results}
\label{sec:experiments:p2p}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:p2p_results} converts the proposal-recall ceiling (overlap@3) into strict top-1 executable accuracy via the P2P decoder (\S\ref{sec:method:p2p}).
On Qwen2.5-VL backbones, cross-layer consensus re-ranking (``+Z-P2P'') recovers a modest but consistent share of the headroom under the strict protocol, with damage $\le 1.5\%$ on SS-v2 and $\le 3.2\%$ on the tiny-target SS-Pro; the local Gaussian-inversion stage adds recovery but, as expected, raises the damage rate on sub-pixel SS-Pro targets, so we report the gated variant and treat the recovery/damage trade-off as an analysis result rather than a free lunch.
On Qwen3-VL backbones, whose native serializer is already precise, the posterior-constrained native-decoding gate (``+Z-P2P-native'') is the effective variant: it defers to the backbone's continuous coordinate whenever the ZwerGe posterior endorses the same region, and otherwise falls back to the P2P point. This closes most of the negative-optimization gap on overlap@1 (GUI-Owl-1.5-8B SS-Pro: $-6.08\to-2.03$\,pp; UI-Venus-1.5-8B SS-Pro: $-4.17\to+0.51$\,pp, i.e.\ from harmful to neutral-positive) and, more strikingly, recovers the strict hit@1 that patch-level quantization had destroyed (UI-Venus-1.5-8B SS-Pro hit@1: $48.9\to66.2$; GUI-Owl-1.5-8B SS-Pro: $52.6\to66.6$) --- turning a harmful retrofit into a neutral-or-positive one precisely where the bottleneck is mildest.

\section{Ablations}
\paragraph{Setup.}
We ablate three design choices on UI-TARS-1.5-7B using a fast LoRA-probe variant, separate from the final CrossAttn probes in \S\ref{sec:experiments:main}.
All variants are trained on 50k samples and evaluated at checkpoint~800.
We vary:
\textbf{(i) GT label type}, binary box labels vs.\ anisotropic Gaussian labels;
\textbf{(ii) fusion scorer}, Readiness-MLP vs.\ CosMeta from \S\ref{sec:method:stage2};
and \textbf{(iii) probe layer range}, varied within L18--L27.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows three trends.
First, fusion matters most on SS-Pro: replacing Readiness-MLP with CosMeta improves Binary GT from A0 to A2 by $+3.2$~pp, indicating that sample-dependent cosine weighting is more effective than hand-crafted readiness features.
Second, GT label type interacts with fusion: Gaussian labels slightly help under Readiness (A0$\to$A1) but lag behind Binary under CosMeta at this early checkpoint (A2 vs.\ A3), suggesting soft targets need longer convergence or different fusion calibration.
Third, layer range is critical.
Removing the deepest layers L26--L27 improves SS-Pro by $+3.5$~pp (A3$\to$A4), consistent with our finding that late layers are more serialization-oriented than spatially informative.
Truncating further to L18--L23 sharply hurts performance (A4$\to$A5), identifying L18--L25 as the best trade-off band.
