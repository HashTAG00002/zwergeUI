\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments:setup}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on five GUI grounding benchmarks spanning desktop, web, and mobile platforms.
\textbf{ScreenSpot-Pro}~\citep{screenspotpro} is a high-resolution desktop grounding benchmark with fine-grained domain categories (Development, Creative, CAD, Scientific, Office, OS); it is the primary benchmark for our analysis (\S\ref{sec:analysis}) and main results.
\textbf{ScreenSpot-v2}~\citep{Os-atlas} covers mobile, desktop, and web screenshots at moderate resolution.
\textbf{OSWorld-G}~\citep{xie2025scaling} provides GUI grounding samples drawn from real computer task executions, categorized by task type (Text Matching, Element Recognition, Layout Understanding, Fine-grained Manipulation). We remove refusal samples from the evaluation set and report results on the remaining non-refusal subset.
\textbf{MMBench-GUI}~\citep{mmbench} is a multi-platform benchmark covering Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, and Web.
\textbf{UI-Vision}~\citep{ui-vision} evaluates grounding across Basic, Functional, Spatial, and Layout task types; we report the weighted average (Avg) over all 5{,}790 samples.



\paragraph{Metric.}
We report \textbf{overlap@$k$}: a click is counted as correct if a $1\times1$-patch box centered at the predicted point (half-width $0.5$ patch, i.e., $14$--$16$\,px depending on backbone patch size) intersects the ground-truth bounding box, taking the best of the top-$k$ candidates.
This differs from the standard \textbf{hit@1} metric (point strictly inside the box, single prediction) used on public leaderboards; overlap@$k$ is strictly easier and the two numbers are \emph{not} directly comparable.
For autoregressive \textbf{baseline} models we report overlap@1: the model emits a single $(x,y)$ coordinate via standard decoding, and we evaluate whether the resulting one-patch box touches the ground-truth box.
For \textbf{+ ZwerGe} we report overlap@3: our probe produces a full posterior over all visual-token patches in a single forward pass (no autoregressive decoding), from which we extract the top-3 connected-component peaks (\S\ref{sec:method}) as three candidate points, and count a hit if any of the three overlaps the ground-truth box.
Because all three candidates are read off the same forward pass, reporting overlap@3 does not add extra inference cost relative to overlap@1 for \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} (no additional decoding step is performed), but it is a different — and less strict — comparison than the single-candidate overlap@1 used for the baseline rows, and the two protocols should not be read as a matched ablation of the retrofit alone.
We report both protocols explicitly, as-is, in every table below rather than normalizing them to a common metric.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We retrofit \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} onto four GUI agents: \textbf{GUI-Owl-7B}~\citep{ye2025guiowl} and \textbf{UI-TARS-1.5-7B}~\citep{qin2025uitars} (Qwen2.5-VL backbone, 28 layers) and \textbf{GUI-Owl-1.5-8B}~\citep{xu2026mobile} and \textbf{UI-Venus-1.5-8B}~\citep{team2026ui} (Qwen3-VL backbone, 36 layers). Each backbone is kept completely frozen throughout both training stages; only the probe and fusion heads are updated.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Stage 1 (A7, \S\ref{sec:method:stage1}) trains 14–18 independent CrossAttn probes per model on 200k grounding samples for 1 epoch. Stage 2 (A8, \S\ref{sec:method:stage2}) loads the Stage 1 checkpoint and trains only the Cross-Layer Fusion head (12 active layers, fusion\_lora\_rank=128, context\_rank=64, $\lambda{=}0.2$) for 1 epoch on the same data. Inference uses the BFS centroid decode strategy with a $0.3{\times}\max$ threshold; no autoregressive decoding is performed.

Tables~\ref{tab:main_sspro}–\ref{tab:main_uivision} report overlap@1 for baseline rows and overlap@3 for \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} rows (\S\ref{sec:experiments:setup}), for all four backbone models.
Each backbone occupies two adjacent rows: the original model (baseline, overlap@1) followed by its \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} retrofitted version (\textbf{+ ZwerGe}, overlap@3).
Because the two rows use different protocols, the absolute deltas discussed below conflate the retrofit itself with the relaxation from a single autoregressive prediction to the best of three patch-posterior candidates read from one forward pass, and should not be read as an ablation of the retrofit in isolation.
Since \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} is an incremental retrofit rather than a standalone agent, we do not include external leaderboard entries in the main comparison — the primary question is whether retrofitting a frozen backbone with our grounding heads provides a consistent improvement under this protocol.

\textbf{Qwen2.5-VL backbones benefit more than Qwen3-VL backbones.}
On ScreenSpot-Pro, GUI-Owl-7B (Qwen2.5-VL) gains $+16.82$~pp overall (52.12$\to$68.94) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B gains $+11.95$~pp (51.11$\to$63.06); GUI-Owl-1.5-8B (Qwen3-VL) gains only $+1.77$~pp (74.83$\to$76.60) and UI-Venus-1.5-8B gains $+4.75$~pp (70.46$\to$75.21).
This pattern is consistent with the our hypothesis: Qwen3-VL models are trained on substantially more diverse grounding supervision, yielding stronger end-to-end coordinate serialization and a smaller gap between intermediate spatial quality and final-layer output.
When the backbone already converts spatial intent to coordinates reliably, bypassing the final-layer decoder recovers less headroom, even after the top-3 relaxation.

\textbf{High-resolution layouts remain a challenge for patch-level decoding.}
ScreenSpot-Pro is the most demanding benchmark because targets are often single-pixel or sub-icon elements on $2{,}560{\times}1{,}440$ or larger screenshots.
\textsc{ZwerGe-UI} replaces pixel-precise coordinate regression with a patch-level posterior over the visual token grid, so the minimum localization granularity is one patch rather than a single pixel.
On ScreenSpot-Pro, several domain categories (CAD, Creative) show the smallest gains even under the top-3 relaxation, whereas moderate-resolution benchmarks (ScreenSpot-v2, MMBench-GUI) are more uniformly positive.
The patch granularity is an architectural trade-off: it enables frozen-backbone readout without any coordinate vocabulary but introduces a resolution ceiling that limits gains on the hardest high-resolution domains, a ceiling that additional candidates alone cannot fully remove.

\textbf{Spatial grounding tasks benefit most.}
UI-Vision decomposes grounding into Basic, Functional, Spatial, and Layout subtypes.
Baseline models are weakest on Spatial (8.73--40.41\%), and \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} provides the largest absolute improvements there: GUI-Owl-7B improves Spatial from 11.52 to 46.25\% ($+34.73$~pp) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B from 8.73 to 30.08\% ($+21.35$~pp).
This is the task type most directly probing whether a model has formed a spatial posterior over the screen, which is precisely the signal read by our intermediate-layer probes.

\section{Ablations}
\paragraph{Setup.}
We ablate three design choices on UI-TARS-1.5-7B using a fast LoRA-probe variant, separate from the final CrossAttn probes in \S\ref{sec:experiments:main}.
All variants are trained on 50k samples and evaluated at checkpoint~800.
We vary:
\textbf{(i) GT label type}, binary box labels vs.\ anisotropic Gaussian labels;
\textbf{(ii) fusion scorer}, Readiness-MLP vs.\ CosMeta from \S\ref{sec:method:stage2};
and \textbf{(iii) probe layer range}, varied within L18--L27.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows three trends.
First, fusion matters most on SS-Pro: replacing Readiness-MLP with CosMeta improves Binary GT from A0 to A2 by $+3.2$~pp, indicating that sample-dependent cosine weighting is more effective than hand-crafted readiness features.
Second, GT label type interacts with fusion: Gaussian labels slightly help under Readiness (A0$\to$A1) but lag behind Binary under CosMeta at this early checkpoint (A2 vs.\ A3), suggesting soft targets need longer convergence or different fusion calibration.
Third, layer range is critical.
Removing the deepest layers L26--L27 improves SS-Pro by $+3.5$~pp (A3$\to$A4), consistent with our finding that late layers are more serialization-oriented than spatially informative.
Truncating further to L18--L23 sharply hurts performance (A4$\to$A5), identifying L18--L25 as the best trade-off band.
