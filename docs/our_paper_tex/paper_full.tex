\begin{abstract}
Autoregressive GUI agents ground instructions by generating screen coordinates, conflating two computations that the final-layer reading leaves undistinguished: locating the target widget in screen space, and serializing that location into coordinate tokens.
We show these computations peak at different transformer depths: spatial grounding is best decoded from intermediate layers and often degrades near the output, while coordinate-token predictability keeps improving in later layers.
We call this mismatch the \textbf{coordinate serialization bottleneck}.
To exploit it, we introduce \textbf{\textsc{ZwerGe-UI}}, a frozen-backbone retrofit that reads intermediate-layer posteriors through lightweight cross-attention probes (${\approx}42$M parameters, $0.5\%$ of an 8B backbone), fuses them with a ${<}200$K-parameter head, and routes each sample between the fused point and the backbone's own coordinate via a training-free confidence gate.
Experiments across 4 GUI agents, 2 backbone families, and 5 benchmarks show that the gate yields small but consistent and statistically significant gains (paired $p{<}0.001$) under the identical strict success-rate protocol, at the cost of a single forward pass.
\end{abstract}
