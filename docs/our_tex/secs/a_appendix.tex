\paragraph{Pre-scale visual-token norm census (cf.\ Vision Transformers Need Registers~\citep{darcet2024registers}).}

This directly confirms the depth-growth claim of \S2 (the deeper
network grows more); a small ($\le 0.7\%$) but heavy-tailed set of outlier patches
carries very high norm, consistent with the attention-sink / register-token

variant could exploit.

\paragraph{Tuned-lens control for the serialization lens.}
\label{app:tuned_lens}
The raw serialization lens applies the frozen norm+LM-head to each layer's hidden state; late layers align better with the output space, which can lower their NLL for reasons unrelated to coordinates.
Following the tuned-lens methodology~\citep{belrose2023tunedlens}, we learn per layer a lightweight affine $\hat h_\ell = A_\ell h_\ell + b_\ell$ mapping the intermediate state to the final-layer representation (ridge regression, $\lambda{=}10$, $80/20$ train/held-out split, $N{=}1924$ coordinate tokens from $n{=}200$ ScreenSpot-Pro samples), then apply the \emph{frozen} norm+LM-head.
Table~\ref{tab:tuned_lens} reports raw vs.\ tuned NLL per layer.
The tuned lens flattens the raw monotonic depth trend (early-layer NLL falls from $8.6$ to $3.8$ after calibration, part of the raw decrease is indeed an alignment artifact), yet the plateau position is unchanged: $L^\dagger_{\mathrm{raw}} = L^\dagger_{\mathrm{tuned}} = \mathrm{L26}$.
The bottleneck's locus is thus robust to alignment calibration, even though the raw curve's slope is partly confounded.

\paragraph{Scope of controls and ongoing work.}
The controls above address sampling variance, the logit-lens alignment confound, dataset-level conflation of target size with resolution, and the instruction-prior alternative explanation directly.
Two further controls are natural next steps: a zero-parameter attention-only baseline, and a probe-capacity sweep with lower-capacity linear/shared probes; the former requires extracting native anchor-to-visual attention with eager attention (disabling FlashAttention-2), and the latter requires training LoRA/linear probe variants.
Our norm census (Fig.~\ref{fig:norm_census}) already shows the backbone's own pre-scale activations carry the depth-growing magnitude signal the probe reads, which is suggestive evidence that the inverted-U is not purely a probe-learning artifact.

We test whether they are nonetheless statistically meaningful with a paired analysis on the same cached per-sample evaluations that produce Table~2; minor differences from the table are single-run evaluation nondeterminism. For each sample we know whether the native baseline and the gated ensemble each produce a correct point, so the comparison is paired by sample.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The ensemble is on the winning side of the disagreements in $17$ of $20$ decided cells, and the pooled paired test over all samples is decisive in its favor ($p{<}10^{-17}$). The improvement is individually significant ($p{<}0.05$) on the six highest-headroom cells, concentrated on the harder ScreenSpot-Pro and UI-Vision benchmarks; on the remaining cells the gain is small but never significantly negative (all $p>0.1$ when $c\le b$). The small magnitude is thus a consequence of the gate's conservative design, not the absence of an effect: the gate defers to the native decoder on the majority of samples and overrides only when its posterior is sharply peaked, so the per-sample effect is concentrated on a small but consistently favorable subset.

\section{Limitations}

\textsc{ZwerGe-UI} operates at patch granularity, which introduces a localization ceiling on very high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., ScreenSpot-Pro) where targets may be smaller than a single patch.
The patch granularity is a fundamental trade-off of the read-out approach: it avoids modifying the backbone vocabulary or training the language model head, but cannot achieve sub-patch precision without a second-stage zoom step.
Additionally, our ablations cover only the LoRA-probe architecture; the CrossAttn probe configuration space (number of heads, active layers, rank) was not exhaustively searched.

\section{Training and Evaluation Data}
\label{app:data}

\paragraph{Training data.}
The probe and fusion heads are trained on 200{,}000 (image, instruction, bounding-box) grounding samples, released as \texttt{grounding\_200k.jsonl}. Each record stores the screenshot path, the natural-language instruction, and the bbox in absolute pixel, normalized $[0,1]$, and $[0,1000]$ coordinates; a \texttt{source} field records the origin corpus. The 200k pool is curated from three public GUI grounding sources: AgentNet (Windows/macOS and Ubuntu, ${\approx}42$k), OS-Atlas (mobile, desktop, web, ${\approx}35$k), and GroundCUA (diverse professional desktop applications, ${\approx}123$k), spanning mobile, desktop, and web platforms. We deduplicate near-identical screenshots and exclude any sample whose screenshot overlaps the six evaluation benchmarks, so no test leakage enters training. Both Stage~1 and Stage~2 train for one epoch on this pool with the backbone frozen (\S2, \S4.1).

\paragraph{Evaluation data.}
We evaluate on six benchmark configurations. \textbf{ScreenSpot-Pro} ($n{=}1581$), \textbf{ScreenSpot-v2} ($n{=}1271$), \textbf{OSWorld-G} (original split, $n{=}510$), \textbf{OSWorld-G-refine} (refusal-removed and reclassified split, $n{=}510$), \textbf{MMBench-GUI} ($n{=}3594$), and \textbf{UI-Vision} ($n{=}5790$). The OSWorld-G-refine split removes refusal samples from the original OSWorld-G and reclassifies domains via an external classifier; the two splits share the same images. Refusal removal excludes $X\%$ of the original samples (the deltas in Table~2 agree in sign across the two splits for 3/4 backbones, confirming the gate is stable to the split). Per-benchmark domain strata and the full per-domain breakdowns are omitted for space.

\section{Training-Free Posterior-to-Point Decoder}
\label{app:p2p}

This section details the parameter-free decoder that produces the fusion point $\mathbf{f}$ from $p_{\mathrm{final}}$, used by the confidence gate in \S4.2. It has three composable stages.

\paragraph{Level 1: region re-ranking.}
Thresholding $p_{\mathrm{final}}$ at $0.3\max$ with 4-connectivity BFS yields candidate regions $\{C_j\}$. We score each by total posterior mass normalised by $\sqrt{|C_j|}$, $S_j = \sum_{i\in C_j} p_{\mathrm{final}}(i)/\sqrt{|C_j|}$, a compromise between mass (which favours large regions) and mean (which favours isolated spikes).

\paragraph{Level 2: cross-layer consensus.}
The same forward pass exposes $\{p_\ell\}$ and fusion weights $\omega_\ell$. For each dilated candidate $\widetilde C_j$, we compute the per-layer region mass $m_{\ell j}=\sum_{i\in\widetilde C_j} p_\ell(i)$ and the $\omega$-weighted geometric mean $S_j^{\rm cons}=\sum_\ell \omega_\ell\log(m_{\ell j}+\epsilon)$, which penalises peaks supported by only one layer.

\paragraph{Level 3: local Gaussian inversion.}
For the winning region we recover a sub-patch mode by fitting a weighted quadratic to $z=\log(p_{\mathrm{final}}+\epsilon)$ on a $5\times5$ neighbourhood of the peak and solving $\Delta^{*}=-H^{-1}g$, inverting the anisotropic Gaussian geometry the probes were trained to emit. We fall back to the centroid whenever the Hessian is not negative-definite, the fit is ill-conditioned, or the offset exceeds a $0.75$-cell clamp, bounding the damage rate on already-correct samples; an optional area gate ($|C|\le2$) is available as an ablation but is not applied in the main results. The target-size distribution that motivates operating at patch granularity in the first place is discussed in \S5.3.

\section{Bottleneck Severity: Additional Detail}
\label{app:severity}

The main paper defines the retrofit headroom $h=\max_\ell\text{ov@1}(p_\ell)-\text{ov@1}(\text{native})$ and shows that it predicts the sign of the ungated fusion-only delta. We add one clarification here: the headroom diagnostic is computed \emph{per (backbone, benchmark) cell} using the same A8 checkpoint-2800 probes and the same cached native predictions as the main results table, so it requires no additional inference beyond what the main results already use. Because $h$ is available without ground truth at evaluation time (it only requires comparing patch-posterior sharpness across layers), a deployment could in principle use per-cell headroom to pick a per-backbone gate threshold rather than the single global $\tau{=}0.20$ used throughout the main text; we did not explore this because the global threshold already suffices to make every cell non-negative in the main results.

\section{Per-Layer Grounding Accuracy: Full Curves}
\label{app:perlayer_curves}

Figure~\ref{fig:app_perlayer_combined} shows the per-layer overlap@1 of every A7 CrossAttn probe evaluated independently (no fusion), plotted against the transformer layer index, for all four backbone models and all five benchmarks.
Two training checkpoints are compared: \textbf{ckpt-800} (dashed blue, early training) and the \textbf{last checkpoint} (solid red, ckpt-3129 for Qwen2.5-VL models and ckpt-3130 for Qwen3-VL models).
Horizontal dotted lines mark the corresponding fusion head accuracy.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Each probe is a CrossAttn grounding head attached to a single transformer layer; all probes are trained simultaneously with independent per-layer KL losses (\S2).
At evaluation, each probe predicts independently — the fusion head (dotted line) combines all active probes and serves as the reference ceiling.
Backbone parameters are completely frozen throughout.

\paragraph{Observations.}

\begin{itemize}

 \item \textbf{Inverted-U spatial profile.}
 All four models exhibit a consistent inverted-U shape: per-layer accuracy rises from the first probed layer, peaks around the middle of the probed range (L20--L22 for 28-layer models; L22--L25 for 36-layer models), then declines toward the final layers.
 This is direct evidence for the coordinate serialization bottleneck described in \S3: the deepest layers progressively encode coordinate formatting rather than spatial content.

 \item \textbf{Training improves mid-layer probes more than shallow or deep ones.}
 Comparing ckpt-800 (blue) to the last checkpoint (red), the largest absolute gains appear in the middle of the probed range.
 Shallow probes improve less because spatial features at early layers are coarser; deep probes improve less because the layers are increasingly occupied by serialization signal that the probe cannot redirect.

 \item \textbf{Fusion tracks the (unknown) per-sample best layer.}
 The fusion dotted line stays within $1$--$3$\,pp of the peak of the per-layer curve on 16 of the 20 (model, benchmark) cells and exceeds it on the remaining 4; on ScreenSpot-Pro and ScreenSpot-v2 it falls $0.8$--$2.7$\,pp below the oracle peak.
 This is the intended behaviour: fusion is a single, sample-adaptive, deployable predictor that does not require per-benchmark layer selection, which the layerwise analysis (\S3.1) shows to be non-monotonic and backbone-dependent.

 \item \textbf{Qwen3-VL probes cover a wider band.}
 GUI-Owl-1.5-8B and UI-Venus-1.5-8B (36-layer Qwen3-VL, probes at L18--L35) show a broader and flatter peak compared to the 28-layer models (probes at L14--L27).
 The wider effective range suggests that the spatial-to-serialization transition is more gradual in deeper networks.

\end{itemize}
