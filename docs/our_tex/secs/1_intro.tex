\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive GUI agents ground instructions by generating screen coordinates, conflating two computations: \emph{locating} the target widget in screen space and \emph{serializing} that location into a coordinate vocabulary.
Prior work has exploited attention maps and prefill-stage signals to aid localization~\citep{xu2025attention,wu2025gui,lin2026happens}, while analyses of vision transformers show that final-layer representations may lose spatial fidelity~\citep{darcet2024registers,lan2024clearclip,chen2025rethinking}.
However, these lines do not ask \emph{where within the prefill computation} spatial grounding is most accessible.
At the same time, GUI-agent training studies reveal a tension between chain-of-thought reasoning and precise localization~\citep{xu2026mobile,yang2026gui,zhou2025gui,zhang2025does}, motivating perception--reasoning decoupling~\citep{zhang2026omegause}.
We operationalize this by freezing the backbone and reading spatial beliefs directly from intermediate activations.

We study this question with lightweight frozen-backbone readouts and reveal a consistent non-monotonic trajectory: spatial grounding improves through early and middle layers, peaks at intermediate depths, and often degrades toward the final layer.
A complementary \emph{serialization lens}---teacher-forced coordinate-token likelihood without additional parameters---shows that coordinate tokens become most predictable in later layers.
This mismatch, which we call the \textbf{coordinate serialization bottleneck}, is not a generic saliency effect: an instruction-switch counterfactual shifts the mid-layer posterior from one target to another, and the bottleneck is most consequential in high-resolution layouts with small targets, where final-layer accuracy is lowest.

Motivated by this, we introduce \textsc{ZwerGe-UI}, a frozen-backbone retrofit for existing GUI agents.
\textsc{ZwerGe-UI} attaches lightweight cross-attention probes to intermediate layers to recover layerwise spatial posteriors, combines them with a small cross-layer fusion head, and then applies a training-free confidence gate that decides, per sample, when to trust the intermediate-layer point over the backbone's own autoregressive coordinate.
The probes serve a dual role: they provide the analytical lens that reveals the bottleneck, and they form the grounding component used at inference.
Importantly, \textsc{ZwerGe-UI} does not modify the agent backbone or its reasoning pathway.

We evaluate on four GUI agents spanning two backbone families, Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL, across five GUI grounding benchmarks, ScreenSpot-Pro, ScreenSpot-v2, OSWorld-G, MMBench-GUI, and UI-Vision, six configurations in total since we evaluate OSWorld-G on both its refusal-removed and original splits.
The strongest spatial readout consistently appears before the final layer.
A training-free confidence gate converts this into consistent grounding gains across all four backbones and six benchmarks (\S\ref{sec:experiments:main}).
The gains are small in magnitude but statistically significant: under a paired test on the samples where the gate and the native decoder disagree, the ensemble is net-positive in the large majority of cells and the pooled test over all samples is decisive ($p{<}0.001$); no cell is significantly worse than the baseline (supplementary).
We read the result as evidence that intermediate-layer signals can complement native decoding, not as a new state-of-the-art grounding system.

In summary, our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
 \item We identify the \textbf{coordinate serialization bottleneck}: intermediate layers encode more accurate instruction-conditioned spatial posteriors than final layers, while later layers better predict coordinate tokens.

 \item We introduce two complementary diagnostic lenses---a frozen-backbone \emph{spatial lens} and a parameter-free \emph{serialization lens}---and show a robust depth mismatch across four GUI agents.

 \item We present \textsc{ZwerGe-UI}, a lightweight frozen-backbone retrofit that recovers intermediate-layer spatial signals through cross-attention probes and a training-free, GT-free confidence gate that routes each sample between the fusion point and the native autoregressive coordinate. Across two backbone families and six benchmarks, all four retrofitted agents improve over their native baselines under the identical strict success-rate protocol, at near-zero added cost.
\end{itemize}
